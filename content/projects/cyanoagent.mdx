## The problem

A general-purpose LLM asked about cyanobacteria gene identification was right 4% of the time.
The knowledge exists across curated biological databases — the model just had no grounded path
to it, and no way to signal when it was guessing.

## What I built

- **Grounded knowledge graph.** A database-backed graph the assistant queries instead of
  recalling, taking gene-identification accuracy from 4% to 96%.
- **Three-tier trust model.** Every fact is marked verified, extracted, or inferred, which
  controls how it propagates through the graph. Guardrails block unverified claims from being
  presented as fact.
- **Data resolution fixes.** Diagnosed and fixed bugs producing incorrect gene and organism
  matches, correcting output accuracy across every supported species.
- **Hypothesis pipeline.** A multi-stage pipeline aggregating evidence across 6 databases and
  using Claude Opus to draft structured research proposals, behind a target-identity
  validation guardrail.

## Why it matters

The accuracy number is the headline, but the trust tiers are the part I care about. A research
assistant that is confidently wrong is worse than no assistant, so the interesting design work
was in making the system able to say which of its claims are load-bearing.
